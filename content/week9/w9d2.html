Lecture 19: Dijkstra’s Algorithm Works#

Warmup#

Recall Dijkstra’s algorithm:

Definition 47 (Dijkstra’s Algo)

  • Input: A connected, simple, positively weighted graph \(G=(V,E)\) and a starting vertex \(s\in V\).

  • Body: Compute shortest distances from \(s\) in \(G\).

    1. Assign \(s\) distance 0, \(d[s]=0\), and every other vertex \(d[u]=\infty\).

    2. Initialize \(F=V\) and \(D=\{\}=\phi\).

    3. While \(F\neq \phi\):

      • 3a. Let \(x\in F\) be such that \(d[x]\) is minimum.

      • 3b. For \(\{x,y\}\in E\):

        • \(d[y]=\min\left\{ d[y], d[x]+w(\{x,y\})\right\}\)

      • 3c. Remove \(x\in F\) and add \(\{x,d[x]\}\) to \(D\).

Proof of Correctness for Dijkstra’s#

For the rest of lecture, assume \(G=(V,E)\) is a connected, simple, positively weighted graph.

Theorem 29

Let \(G\) and some starting vertex \(s\) be inputted to Dijkstra’s algorithm. Then, the output will contain the shortest distances between \(s\) and every other vertex in \(G\).

To prove this theorem, we will prove a couple other results first.

Lemma 6

For all \(v\in V\), at any time \(d[v]\geq d(s,v)\) where \(d[v]\) is the current distance label under Dijkstra’s and \(d(s,v)\) is the shortest distance between \(v\) and \(s\).

Proof. We will proceed by induction on the iteration number.

Base case: At initialization, \(d[s]=0\) and \(d[u]=\infty\) for all \(u\neq s\). Since \(G\) is simple, \(d(s,s)=0\) os \(d[s]\geq d(s,s)\). For all the other vertices, infinity is definitely at least as big as the shortest path. So, \(d[u]=\infty\geq d(s,u)\) as well. So, at the beginning, the claim holds.

Inductive Hypothesis: Assume \(d[v]\geq d(s,v)\) for all \(v\in V\) up to when the \(k\)-th vertex distance is changed by Dijkstra’s.

Induct: Consider the \(k+1\)-st vertex to get its distance label changed for some \(x_{k+1}\in V\). Originally, \(d[x_{k+1}]=\infty\). At some point, \(d[x_{k+1}]\) changed to \(d[u]+w(\{u,x_{k+1}\})\) for some vertex \(u\in D\). By the inductive hypothesis, the claim holds for \(u\) (i.e. \(d[u]\geq d(s,u)\)). Then,

\(\begin{align*} d[x_{k+1}]&=d[u]+w(\{u,x_{k+1}\})\\ &\geq d(s,u)+w(\{u,x_{k+1}\})\\ \end{align*}\)

Either \(d(s,u)+w(\{u,x_{k+1}\})\) is the length of a shortest path from \(s\) to \(x_{k+1}\) or it is longer than a shortest path. So,

\(\begin{align*} d[x_{k+1}]&\geq d(s,u)+w(\{u,x_{k+1}\})\\ &\geq d(s,x_{k+1}) \end{align*}\)

Thus, the claim holds for the \(k+1\)-th vertex change.

Therefore, the claim holds at all points.
