### Paths, Trails, & Walks
Recall our definitions of $uv$-paths, trails, and walks. We add a new adjective to these.

````{prf:definition} Open vs. Closed
With regard to $uv$-paths, trails, and walks, if the starting and ending vertex are the same ($u=v$), we say it is *closed*. If the starting and ending vertex are different ($u\neq v$), it is *open*.
````

````{prf:definition} Cycle vs. Circuit
A *circuit* is a closed $uv$ trail. A *cycle* is a closed $uv$ path.
````

````{prf:theorem}
:label: walkpath
If there is an open $uv$-walk, then there is an open $uv$-path.
````
````{prf:proof}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a graph with an open $uv$-walk $W=(\{v_1,v_2\},\{v_2,v_3\},\dots)$ on vertices $V_W=(v_1,v_2,\dots, v_m)$ such that $W$ is a *minimum length* out of all the open $uv$-walks in $G$. If $W$ is already a path, then we are done. So, assume $W$ is not a path. 

Since $W$ is not a path, there is a repeated vertex. Consider the repeated vertex. Let $v_i=v_j$ for $v_i$ and $v_j$ in $V_W$ ($1\leq i<j\leq m$). Remove every edge in between $\{v_{i-1}, v_i\}$ and $\{v_j=v_i, v_{j+1}\}$ from $W$. Call this new open $uv$-walk $W'$ and note $W'=(\{v_1,v_2\},\{v_2,v_3\}, \dots, \{v_{i-1},v_i\},\{v_j=v_i,v_{j+1}\},\dots)$.

If $W'$ is a path, great! We are done. 

If $W'$ is __not__ a path, then $W'$ is an open $uv$-walk of shorter length than $W$. This contradicts the assumption that $W$ is of minimum length.

Therefore, there is an open $uv$-path when there is an open $uv$-walk.
````

There is a simple explanation of what we're doing. Firstly, we are taking a some minimum walk from $u$ to $v$:

We are removing the extra dilly-dally:

And considering a more direct route:

Either this new route is a path or we've violated the assumptions we've made.

A nice corollary to this theorem relates to connectedness:
````{prf:corollary}
If there exists an open $uv$-walk from every pair of vertices in a graph $G$, $G$ is connected.
````
````{prf:proof}
We just proved every open in {prf:ref}`walkpath` that if there is an open $uv$-walk then there is an open $uv$-path. So, if there exists an open $uv$-walk from every pair of vertices in a graph $G$, there is an open $uv$-path between them too and $G$ is connected.
````

### What about in digraphs?

These ideas of connectedness also extend to digraphs with some tweaking.

````{prf:definition} Directed Paths, Trails, & Walks
A *directed path* is a sequence of arcs $\mathcal{P}=((v_1,v_2), (v_2,v_3), \dots, (v_{m-1},v_m))$ containing a sequence of distinct nodes $\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}}=(v_1, v_2, \dots, v_m)$ such that each node $v_i\in \mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}}$ in at most two arcs of $P$. We call $v_1\in \mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}}$ the starting node and $v_m\in \mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{P}}$ the ending node.

A *directed trail* is a directed path, but nodes may be repeated.

A *directed walk* is a directed path, but we can repeat nodes and arcs.

A directed *uv*-path, trail, or walk means the starting node is $u$ and the ending node is $v$.

*Open* and *closed* mean the same as before: $u\neq v$ for the former and $u=v$ for the latter.
````

While these definitions extend nicely, connectedness in digraph is broadly defined in two categories: *weak* connectivity and *strong* connectivity.

````{prf:definition} Weak vs. Strong Connectedness
A digraph is *strongly connected* if for every pair of distinct nodes $u$ and $v$, there is a $uv$-directed path and a $vu$-directed path.

A digraph is *weakly connected* if by replacing every arc with an edge, we obtain a connected graph.
````
