### Proof of Correctness (continued)

We prove another result before finalizing our proof of correctness for Dijkstra's.
````{prf:lemma}
Let $P$ be a shortest path from $u$ to $v$ with some vertex $w$ in $P$. The path $Q$ from $u$ to $w$ along $P$ is also a shortest path (from $u$ to $w$).
````

````{prf:proof}
Let $Q$ be the subpath from $u$ to $w$ along $P$. Let $P_1$ be the subpath from $w$ to $v$ along $P$. Assume there is some shortest $uw$-path $Q'$. 

Note that $Q$ with $P_1$ is the $uv$-path $P$ and $Q'$ with $P_1$ is some $uv$-path $R$. Since $Q'$ is a shortest $uw$-path, $w(Q')\leq w(Q)$. Likewise, since $P$ is a shortest $uv$-path, $w(R)\leq w(P)$. Since $w(Q)+w(P_1)=w(P)$ and $w(Q')+w(P_1)=w(R)$, we can substitute into this inequality:

$\begin{align*}
w(R)\leq w(P) &\implies w(Q')+w(P_1)\leq w(Q)+w(P_1)\\
&\implies w(Q')\leq w(Q)
\end{align*}$

So, $w(Q')\leq w(Q)$ and $w(Q)\leq w(Q')$ meaning $w(Q')=w(Q)$. Thus, $Q$ is a shortest $uw$-path.
````

Our final result will show that Dijkstra's indeed finds the shortest distances:

````{prf:theorem}
Let $u\in V$. When $u$ is placed into the set $D$, $d[u]=d(s,u)$.
````
````{prf:proof}
Consider the point where we add $u$ to $D$. 

First, note that $d[u]\leq d[v]$ for all $v\in F$ by Step 3a.

Second, note that $d[u]$ is not changed after adding $u$ to $D$. The only way $d[u]$ could be changed is if there were some $v\in F$ such that Step 3b results in $d[u]=\min\{d[u],d[v]+w(\{u,v\})\}=d[v]+w(\{u,v\})$. This is impossible since $d[u]\leq d[v]$ by Step 3a and $w(\{u,v\})>0$ by assumption.

We will proceed with induction on the number of vertices in $D$.

__Base Case__: The first vertex to be put in $D$ is $s$! Since $G$ is simple, $d(s,s)=0$. By Step 1 of Dijkstra's, $d[s]=0$ when $s$ is added to $D$. So, the claim holds for the first vertex added to $D$.

__Inductive Hypothesis__: Assume $d[u]=d(s,u)$ for the first $k$ vertices added to $D$.

__Induct__: Let $\{x_1=s,x_2,x_3,\dots,x_k\}$ be the first $k$ vertices added to $D$ and $x_{k+1}$ be the next vertex to be added (i.e. $d[x_{k+1}]$ is minimum in $F$). Let $P$ be a shortest path between $s$ and $x_{k+1}$.

Assume $y\in V$ is the closest vertex in $P$ to $x_{k+1}$ such that $d[y]=d(s,y)$. By assumption, any vertex $v\in V$ between $y$ and $x_{k+1}$ along $P$ must have $d[v]>d(s,v)$.

- Case 1: $y=x_{k+1}$. Then, $d[x_{k+1}]=d(s,x_{k+1})$ and we are done.

- Case 2: $y\neq x_{k+1}$. Then, there is some vertex $y'$ after $y$ on $P$. We know $d[y]=d(s,y)\leq d(s,x_{k+1})\leq d[x_{k+1}]$ by our lemmas above and the fact that edgeweights are positive in $G$. If $d[y]=d[x_{k+1}]$, we have our result. So, assume $d[y]<d[x_{k+1}]$. By the choice of $x_{k+1}$, $d[x_{k+1}]$ was minimum in $F$ so $y$ must have been in $D$ at this point. In particular, $\{y,y'\}$ was an edge that was already considered such that by Step 3b, 

$\begin{align*}
d[y']&\leq d[y]+w(\{y,y'\})
\end{align*}$

By choice of $y$, $d[y]+w(\{y,y'\})=d(s,y)+w(\{y,y'\})$. Also, by our shortest subpaths lemma, $d(s,y)+w(\{y,y'\})=d(s,y')$. So, we have shown $d[y']\leq d(s,y')$ and by our first lemma $d[y']\geq d(s,y')$. This means $d[y']=d(s,y')$. This contradicts the choice of $y$ as the closest vertex to $x_{k+1}$ in $P$ with this equality. So, $d[y]<d[x_{k+1}]$ is false and $d[x_{k+1}]=d(s,x_{k+1})$.

So, our claim holds for the $k+1$-th vertex added to $D$. Therefore, our claim holds for every vertex added to $D$.
````
